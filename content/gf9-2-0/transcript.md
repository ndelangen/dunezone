### Notes, left column

```text
Dune has long been celebrated for its unique blend of
strategy, negotiation, and theme, and this edition makes
it easier than ever to learn and enjoy what makes the
game so distinctive.
This edition refines and streamlines long-standing
systems, clarifies edge cases, and preserves the depth
and tension the game is known for, while addressing
areas that have generated discussion over decades of
gaming.
When Dune was first released in 1979, it had a single
ruleset with several optional additions. By 2019, these
had been divided into Basic and Advanced rules. This
edition aims to return to one unified system that reflects
how most people actually play, while keeping the best
elements of both approaches.
The biggest change is combat. The original system was
elegant and intuitive, while the advanced version added
spice as an extra factor but became more complex and
sometimes clunky. This edition introduces a revised
combat system that keeps the original simplicity while
adding a streamlined spice element to preserve that
extra layer of strategy. For veteran players who prefer
it, the original advanced combat ("Spice Dialing") is still
available as a variant.
```

### Notes, middle column

```text
Another major update is the Karama card. Previously,
it tried to cover too many effects, leading to confusion
and inconsistent rulings. It has now been split into
two cards, Karama and Solaris. The Karama effect has
been simplified so it cancels just one key faction ability,
making gameplay clearer and easier to manage, while
Solaris contains the additional effects that were part of
the original Karama card.
Some terminology and naming have been improved for
clarity, theme, and consistency. For example, "projectile"
has been changed to "kinetic," some locations have been
corrected, and many faction abilities have been renamed
to better reflect their purpose.
Lastly, factions have been lightly updated based on years
of player feedback and tournament data. Most remain
very close to their original design, with small tweaks to
improve balance and playability.
Overall, the goal is to keep what has worked for decades
while making the game smoother, clearer, and more
accessible.
—The Future Pastimes team.
```

### Game board and choose factions

```text
Game Board
Place all Spice tokens in the Spice Bank. Shuffle the Spice
deck, Storm deck, and Treachery deck and place them
face down next to the game board. Played cards will
be piled face up next to the decks as discard piles and
reshuffled to restock those decks as necessary.
Choose Factions
Players now choose factions. Here are a couple of ways
for players to choose their factions:
1. Use the faction cards from the Bene Gesserit
Prediction deck to randomly determine which
Faction each player will play.
Shuffle the cards and deal 1 card to each player.
2. Each player randomly draws a Round Prediction card
from the Bene Gesserit Prediction deck and selects
a faction in descending card order, starting with the
lowest card.
Players may swap factions with each other, or a faction
that wasn't chosen, at this point if they wish.
Dune is widely considered best at 6 players, but below
are some recommendations for fewer players:
Two & three-player games
For a three-player game, recommended factions are
Atreides, Harkonnen, and Fremen.
For a two-player game, try Atreides vs Harkonnen,
or play 2 factions each (select factions normally
as described above). In either case, the number of
strongholds needed to win is increased to 4. There are no
alliances.
Four & Five-player games
For 4 players, consider leaving out both the Spacing
Guild and Bene Gesserit.
For 5 player games try playing without the Bene
Gesserit.
```

### Player setup

```text
Player Setup
Players take their Faction Screens and Faction Sheets
and set up their factions as follows: .
1. POSITIONS
Randomize seating order. Players place their Faction
Marker on the player circle closest to their Faction
Screen and seat at the table.
2. TREACHERY
Deal cards from the Treachery deck face down to
each player equal to the number indicated by their
Faction Sheet.
3. TRAITORS
Remove the cards for all factions that are not in play
from the Traitor deck. Shuffle the cards thoroughly
and deal each player 4 cards.
Each player then secretly selects 1 card to keep by
placing their chosen card face down behind their
Faction Screen, returning the other cards face down
to the bottom of the Traitor deck A player may
choose a Traitor card matching one of their own
leaders, for a guaranteed safe leader.
4. SPICE
Give each player spice from the Spice Bank equal
to the number indicated by their Faction Sheet. All
spice holdings should be kept secret behind Faction
Screens.
5. FORCES
Place each player's forces on the board as indicated
by their Faction Sheet. All remaining forces are
```

```text
The sides had been chosen and the final confrontation was at hand.
On paper, the Harkonnens, Emperor, Spacing Guild and Landsraad
alliance seemed overwhelming when compared to the Fremen forces that
Paul could muster. But Paul had a few surprises of his own ready. Not all
of the alliance force was on the planet, only enough deemed sufficient to
stamp out the Fremen uprising. The remainder of the force was in orbit
as an emergency reserve. Paul realized that by threatening to destroy all
spice, he had the leverage to force the Spacing Guild to his side. The final
result of this switch meant that none of the reserve would land on the
planet because, of course, the troops were on Spacing Guild spaceships.
That reduced the strength imbalance considerably. Now he only had to
deal with the advance force which was conveniently in camp preparing
for their campaign.
Calling upon his newly realized powers, Paul summoned a tremendous
sand storm to cover his ambush. Quietly and quickly he distributed his
troops behind the Shield Wall protecting the Imperial forces and waited
for the right moment. As soon as the sand storm passed over the wall and
into the camp, Paul used his family atomics to blow a gaping hole in the
wall. The Fremen poured through riding on the backs of the sandworms!
The surprise was complete and the battle over quickly. Paul had regained
his rightful title to Dune. So ends the first book of the Dune series.
```

### Terms, left column

```text
Available: Your leaders (typically behind your Screen)
that are not dead or captured, nor have battled in a
different territory that round.
Being Formed: Battle Plans are not finalized until they
are ready to be revealed. Effects can cause a player to
change their Battle Plans before revealing (e.g., Battle
Prescience, Voice, Truthtrance, Karama).
Bribe: Spice paid to a non-ally as part of a deal. This
spice is not available immediately, but is collected in the
Mentat Pause phase.
Captured: Any Leader disc of a faction that is held
by another faction (e.g., Harkonnen Capture Leaders
advantage, or Tleilaxu Ghola advantage). Does not
apply to Duke Vidal while held by Moritani or Ecaz's ally
through the Ecaz ambassador.
Card Knowledge: Having information about specific
cards or types of cards, of any kind: Treachery, Traitor,
Prediction, Nexus, etc. For example, cards in hand, cards
sold in auction, Ixians Market Manipulation advantage,
cards seen and shuffled into a deck.
Carryalls: A faction with forces in Arrakeen and/or
Carthag has a spice collection rate of 3 spice per force
in the Spice Collection phase while collecting spice from
spice blows.
Control: A faction controls a stronghold if they alone
occupy it at the end of the round. This is the only time
control is determined, and it does not carry over into
the next round. Bene Gesserit advisors cannot control a
stronghold, nor affect whether another faction controls
it.
Deploy: An effect that allows you to place forces into
a territory. This does not count as a shipment, and no
Bene Gesserit advisor may accompany it.
Forces: A player's tokens as military units. They can
have differing values (e.g., Fedaykin valued at 2 each,
Suboids at 1/2 each) or different states (e.g., Bene
Gesserit advisors and fighters).
Full-Dial: When a player dials their maximum number
(or strength) of forces in a battle.
In Storm: When the storm covers a sector, the portions
of all territories (and any forces in them) in that sector
are considered "in storm".
Initiative: The active player earliest in storm order. This
player will choose the order of their battles, and win any
ties. Initiative moves to the next player in storm order
when the current one has no battles left.
```

### Terms, right column

```text
Occupancy Rule: When two factions occupy a
stronghold, no other factions may enter that territory
unless an effect allows them to do so.
Occupy: When a faction has forces in a stronghold,
they occupy it. Advisors do not count as occupying a
stronghold.
Opponent: A player with whom you are battling.
Ornithopters: If a faction starts their Force Movement
action with one or more forces in Arrakeen and/or
Carthag, they may move any single group of their forces
up to three territories.
Payment: Spice given to a player due to a faction
advantage (e.g., paying Emperor for purchasing
Treachery cards), which would then go behind their
[[SCAN UNCLEAR p36-l064]] Faction Screen and is available for immediate use. Bribe
are not considered payments.
Pocket Card: The Treachery card(s) dealt to a player
during Setup.
Rival: Any player with whom you are not allied.
Secret Information: Any knowledge pertaining to
hidden information: card knowledge, identity of face
down tokens, etc.
Secretly: Without revealing to other players. If you
secretly select a card, you are choosing it without others
seeing what you choose. If you are secretly showing or
revealing something to another player, they get to see
it but others do not (though all players get to know this
transaction is taking place).
Sector: One of the 18 wedge-like divisions around the
board used for determining the location of the storm's
effect. Polar Sink is not considered in any sector.
Set Aside: Placed near the play area for public view, but
not considered "in play" or controlled by any player.
Slot: Virtual space on Battle Wheel for weapon/defense.
Storm Order: Proceeding counter-clockwise, starting
with the First Player when applicable.
Stronghold Blocking: Moving into a stronghold when
[[SCAN UNCLEAR p36-l087]] another faction is there, thereby blocking other factio
[[SCAN UNCLEAR p36-l088]] (particularly one poised to win) from entering. This i
often done as part of a deal, but doesn't need to be.
[[SCAN UNCLEAR p36-l090]] Supply: A pool of available tokens, cards, or resourc
[[SCAN UNCLEAR p36-l091]] not considered part of a player's hand, but neverthel
under their control.
Territory: A space on the map (board) of Dune.
Territories can span several sectors. There are four
different types of territories: desert, rock, stronghold,
and Polar Sink.
```
